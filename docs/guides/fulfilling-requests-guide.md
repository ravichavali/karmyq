# Helping Others

Fulfilling requests is the heart of Karmyq. When you help a neighbor, you earn karma and build trust.

---

## Finding Requests

**The Community Feed** — Your main feed shows open requests, sorted by your preferences and trust connections.

**Your Preferences** — Set which request types you're interested in and the feed prioritizes those.

**The Curated Feed** — Uses the Skills tags in your profile to help rank requests, alongside trust
connections, community relevance, urgency, and activity. Add or update them in **Profile → About You →
Skills**; the "matched to" hint shows which skill Karmyq recognized.

**Two places, one feed** — You'll see the *same* canonical request cards on your **Dashboard Home**
and on each **community's Home tab**. The "Needs your response" band — decisions you owe (offers to
accept, exchanges to mark done, completed exchanges to rate) — lives on the **Helping tab**, not in
either feed, so browsing asks stays separate from acting on commitments. A community's tab adds
**community texture** below the requests — a "this week" activity summary (exchanges completed, new
members, recent helpers) and the occasional story of a neighbour's first exchange. Either way, each card shows the specifics of the
ask — a ride's pickup and dropoff, a move's floors, a tech request's device — so you know what you're
committing to before you offer.

The former standalone request feed now redirects to Dashboard Home, so the live browsing surfaces are
Dashboard Home, Community Home, and the community open-asks view reached from the weekly pulse.

**You only see what you can open.** Browsing a community's requests shows exactly the asks whose
detail page you could open: asks shared with your community or a sister community, asks shared with
the trust network or the whole platform, and asks you are already involved in. A community's feed,
weekly pulse and open-asks view are for its current members. If you leave a community, they close to
you at once.

## Standing Offers

A standing offer says "I can help with this" before anyone asks: a skill you have or a tool you're
happy to lend. Post one from the **Help Offers** page (`/offers`) and pick the community it's for. A standing offer stays available after an exchange it backed completes, until you withdraw it or it expires.

Members of that community see it, and so do you. Nobody outside the community can see it. If you
leave the community, you still see your own offer, but its members are the only other people who can
find it. Only you can change or withdraw your offer.

**Filtering the offers list.** The **Status** filter starts on **Active**: offers someone can still take up.
Choose **Matched** or **Withdrawn** to see that history, or **All** to see every status together. All shows
exactly what the other filters would show combined. It never shows an offer from a community you are not in,
and it never shows another member's expired *active* offer (your own stay visible to you, marked *Expired*).
Matched and withdrawn offers stay in the history even after they expire, with an "Offer expired" note. You can
combine any status with a **Category**.

## Opening a Request

A community's open-asks view (reached from its weekly pulse) lists every open ask across the community — including your own and ones you've already offered on, so the count is always reachable. Those cards don't offer inline; **opening a card is the action path**. Tapping one opens the request's **detail page**, which shows the ask and the single action available to you: **Offer to Help** (or **Offer service** for a service request) when you're eligible, **"waiting for the requester"** when you've already offered, **"This is your ask"** for your own, or a finite note when the ask is completed, cancelled, matched, or expired. Eligibility is decided server-side before the page renders, so you never tap an Offer button that fails. If you took part in a completed exchange on the ask, the page also offers a private rating and a vouch for your partner's skills.

## Accepting a Request

1. Click on a request to open its detail page and see the full ask
2. Check the description, location, timing, and effort involved
3. Click **Offer to Help** if you can help
4. Your offer goes to the requester to accept; track it in the **Helping** tab

**Expired requests take no new offers.** Once a request passes its expiration date, you can't offer on it, and an offer you made earlier can no longer be accepted. An offer still waiting on an expired request is closed for you within about an hour, and the standing offer it held is free again. You can also withdraw it yourself sooner. An expired standing offer can't back a new offer either, and a completed exchange frees the standing offer for the next ask.

**Who still sees an expired standing offer.** Once a standing offer passes its expiry, your neighbours no longer see it on the Help Offers page — but **you** still do, so you know it has lapsed, for about a week: the daily cleanup permanently removes an offer once it has been marked expired for seven days. On your list it reads **Expired** instead of Active. An offer that is already part of an exchange keeps its own status (for example **Matched**) and adds an **Offer expired** note: your commitment carries on, and nothing about it is cancelled or completed because the offer expired. Seeing it does not make it usable — an expired offer still can't be attached to a new request. Matched and withdrawn offers also stay in those lists after their expiry passes; once an offer has been marked expired, the same week-later cleanup removes it.

**An offer you're already using elsewhere can't be used twice.** If you attach the same standing offer to two requests at once, only the first goes through. The second is refused as "Offer is not active". That holds only while an exchange is in progress: once it completes, the offer is free to use again.

## During the Help

Coordinate via Karmyq messages or however you've agreed. Be on time. If something comes up and you can't follow through, tell them promptly in the inline conversation so they can find someone else.

Your reliability is part of your reputation.

---

## Completing the Request

After the help is done:

1. The requester marks it as **Fulfilled**
2. Karma transfers from their balance to yours
3. Your trust score is updated
4. You both get an **Exchange completed** notification. It opens the request page, where you can rate
   the exchange privately and vouch for skills you saw. Only the two people in an exchange see each
   other there.

---

## What Makes a Good Helper

- **Read the full request** before accepting — know what you're agreeing to
- **Don't over-commit** — help fewer people well than accept everything and complete nothing
- **Communicate** — if anything changes, tell the requester
- **Show up** — reliability is your most important reputation factor
