# Helping Others

Fulfilling requests is the heart of Karmyq. When you help a neighbor, you earn karma and build trust.

---

## Finding Requests

**The Community Feed** — Your main feed shows open requests, sorted by your preferences and trust connections.

**Your Preferences** — Set which request types you're interested in and the feed prioritizes those.

**The Curated Feed** — Uses the Skills tags in your profile to help rank requests, alongside trust
connections, community relevance, urgency, and activity. Add or update them in **Profile → About You →
Skills**; the "matched to" hint shows which skill Karmyq recognized.

**Two places, one feed** — You'll see the *same* canonical request cards on your **Dashboard Home**
and on each **community's Home tab**. The "Needs your response" band — decisions you owe (offers to
accept, exchanges to mark done, completed exchanges to rate) — lives on the **Helping tab**, not in
either feed, so browsing asks stays separate from acting on commitments. A community's tab adds
**community texture** below the requests — a "this week" activity summary (exchanges completed, new
members, recent helpers) and the occasional story of a neighbour's first exchange. Either way, each card shows the specifics of the
ask — a ride's pickup and dropoff, a move's floors, a tech request's device — so you know what you're
committing to before you offer.

The former standalone request feed now redirects to Dashboard Home, so the live browsing surfaces are
Dashboard Home, Community Home, and the community open-asks view reached from the weekly pulse.

**You only see what you can open.** Browsing a community's requests shows exactly the asks whose
detail page you could open: asks shared with your community or a sister community, asks shared with
the trust network or the whole platform, and asks you are already involved in. A community's feed,
weekly pulse and open-asks view are for its current members. If you leave a community, they close to
you at once.

## Standing Offers

A standing offer says "I can help with this" before anyone asks: a skill you have or a tool you're
happy to lend. Post one from the **Help Offers** page (`/offers`) and pick the community it's for.

Members of that community see it, and so do you. Nobody outside the community can see it. If you
leave the community, you still see your own offer, but its members are the only other people who can
find it. Only you can change or withdraw your offer.

---

## Opening a Request

A community's open-asks view (reached from its weekly pulse) lists every open ask across the community — including your own and ones you've already offered on, so the count is always reachable. Those cards don't offer inline; **opening a card is the action path**. Tapping one opens the request's **detail page**, which shows the ask and the single action available to you: **Offer to Help** (or **Offer service** for a service request) when you're eligible, **"waiting for the requester"** when you've already offered, **"This is your ask"** for your own, or a finite note when the ask is completed, cancelled, matched, or expired. Eligibility is decided server-side before the page renders, so you never tap an Offer button that fails.

---

## Accepting a Request

1. Click on a request to open its detail page and see the full ask
2. Check the description, location, timing, and effort involved
3. Click **Offer to Help** if you can help
4. Your offer goes to the requester to accept; track it in the **Helping** tab

---

## During the Help

Coordinate via Karmyq messages or however you've agreed. Be on time. If something comes up and you can't follow through, let them know early and cancel the acceptance so someone else can step in.

Your reliability is part of your reputation.

---

## Completing the Request

After the help is done:

1. The requester marks it as **Fulfilled**
2. Karma transfers from their balance to yours
3. Your trust score is updated

---

## What Makes a Good Helper

- **Read the full request** before accepting — know what you're agreeing to
- **Don't over-commit** — help fewer people well than accept everything and complete nothing
- **Communicate** — if anything changes, tell the requester
- **Show up** — reliability is your most important reputation factor
