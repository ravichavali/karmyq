# How Communities Work

Karmyq communities are intentionally small, purpose-built, and founder-configured.

---

## Why Small?

There's a concept in social science called Dunbar's number: humans can maintain stable social relationships with roughly 150 people. Beyond that, we lose the personal knowledge that makes genuine trust possible.

Karmyq communities have size limits by design. In a small community:
- You're likely to recognize names when you see requests
- Reputation matters more (everyone can see your history)
- Accountability is natural
- Generosity is reciprocated within a recognizable group

When communities grow beyond their size limit, they split — creating a new community that carries forward the relationships and trust already built.

---

## The Founder's Role

Every Karmyq community has a founder who sets the rules, culture, and configuration:

- **Request types:** Which kinds of requests are allowed
- **Karma multipliers:** How much karma each request type is worth
- **Trust model:** Depth vs. breadth weighting
- **Membership:** Size limits, visibility, join approval
- **Custom request types:** Via the Schema Manager

---

## Cohorts and Layers

Not all members of a community are equally embedded in it. Karmyq tracks cohort membership — which group of people you joined with — and uses this as part of the trust calculation.

Early members who helped shape the community's culture have deeper roots. New members start fresh, building trust through their first interactions.

---

## Community Splitting

When a community reaches its size limit, it splits. The new community inherits the relationships and culture of the original. Splitting keeps communities small and personal, and lets successful communities naturally propagate.

---

## Visibility Modes

- **Public:** Listed and open — anyone can see and join
- **Members Only:** Joining requires invitation or approval
- **Hybrid:** Discoverable, but joining requires approval

---

## Multiple Communities

You can belong to more than one community. Your karma and trust are tracked separately in each — preserving the integrity of each community's economy and trust network.
